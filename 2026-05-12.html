Singularity Pulse

Tuesday, May 12, 2026

🔥 TOP SIGNAL

Anthropic launched Project Glasswing last month, and the implications are still landing. The premise: their unreleased Claude Mythos Preview can now find software vulnerabilities at a level that surpasses all but the most skilled humans. Over a few weeks, Mythos identified thousands of zero-days across every major operating system and every major browser. It chained four independent bugs into a single exploit that broke both renderer and OS sandboxing. It built a remote-code-execution chain against FreeBSD's NFS server using a 20-gadget ROP payload distributed across packets. That is not a capability demo — that is a working super-hacker, and Anthropic isn't going to ship it.

Why this matters: the offense-defense asymmetry in cybersecurity has been ugly for two decades, and it just got worse. Anthropic's response — a coalition launch with AWS, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, CrowdStrike, Google, JPMorgan, the Linux Foundation, Microsoft, NVIDIA, and Palo Alto Networks — is an acknowledgment that "ship the model, hope for the best" is no longer a defensible posture for offensive-capable systems. The bet is that locking Mythos-class capability behind a vetted defender consortium gives the good guys a 6–18 month head start. Bruce Schneier's read is that this works if and only if the partners actually rebuild their SOCs around Mythos output streams. Most haven't started.

What to watch: whether OpenAI follows suit. GPT-5.5 reportedly cleared the same 32-step end-to-end cyber-attack range three weeks after Mythos. They have not announced a Glasswing equivalent. Whoever ships the defender stack first defines the next decade of enterprise security.

⚡ THE STACK

DeepMind scales AlphaEvolve across science.

Google DeepMind expanded AlphaEvolve, their Gemini-powered coding agent, to materials science, biology, and chip design. The pattern from AlphaFold repeats: package frontier reasoning behind a domain-shaped agent loop and ship to specialists. If the productivity numbers hold up, vertical-AI moats just got harder for startups to defend.

Anthropic raises usage limits, signs SpaceX for compute.

Last Tuesday's twin announcement: Claude users get materially higher limits, and Anthropic is buying compute from SpaceX. Starlink-adjacent data centers were not on most 2026 bingo cards. Watch for similar Anthropic infra deals — the company is clearly setting up for a compute regime measured in tens of GW.

Anthropic + Blackstone + Goldman launch enterprise-AI services co.

Quieter but possibly bigger: a new joint venture with Blackstone, Hellman & Friedman, and Goldman Sachs to provide Claude-powered services to the Fortune 500. This is the consulting-firm play — McKinsey and Accenture should be paying attention. The model lab is moving downstream into deployment muscle.

US government wins pre-launch access to frontier models.

The new Center for AI Standards and Innovation (CAISI) struck agreements with Microsoft, Google, and xAI to test models before public release. Anthropic and OpenAI are not in the announcement. Read this as a soft preview of where compulsory pre-deployment evals are heading — and as an opening for labs that opt in to claim a safety lead.

Gemma 4: DeepMind tightens the open-model squeeze.

"Byte for byte, the most capable open models" is the claim. Open-weight competitive pressure on Llama, Qwen, and DeepSeek keeps intensifying — when Google ships open models they sandbag the competitive moat of everyone whose pitch is "we're open-source." Worth a benchmark spot-check this week.

Distillation hits the long tail.

Cactus Compute's Needle packed Gemini's tool-calling behavior into a 26M-parameter model. On-device tool routing without a frontier API call. The pattern — distill one narrow skill, deploy it cheap — is going to make the next 18 months of cost curves very strange.

📜 PAPERS WORTH KNOWING

WildClawBench: A Benchmark for Real-World, Long-Horizon Agent Evaluation

60 human-authored bilingual multimodal tasks averaging 8 minutes and 20+ tool calls each, run inside real CLI agent harnesses (OpenClaw, Claude Code, Codex, Hermes). Best frontier model — Claude Opus 4.7 under OpenClaw — hits 62.2%. Every other model stays below 60%, and just switching the harness moves a single model by up to 18 points.

Why you care: the "agents are almost there" narrative keeps tripping on its own benchmarks. This one is closer to real deployment than ToolBench or AgentBench, and the harness-sensitivity result is a red flag — capability rankings published today are partly artifacts of scaffolding choices, not just model quality.

Ding et al., Shanghai AI Lab / OpenClaw team. Published May 11, 2026.

The Generalized Turing Test: A Foundation for Comparing Intelligence

Formal framework for "A ≥ B" — B (as distinguisher) can't reliably tell A-imitating-B apart from real B. Builds the order theory (transitivity conditions, equivalence classes) and evaluates pairwise indistinguishability across modern models. Recovers a stratification consistent with existing rankings, but dataset-independent.

Why you care: Poggio's group is taking another swing at "what does it mean to compare intelligence" in a way that doesn't bottom out in benchmark gaming. If this comparator becomes a training objective, it changes the data-curation calculus for the next generation of frontier runs.

Mitropolsky, Hong, Neumarker, Rimoldi, Poggio. MIT. Published May 11, 2026.

The First Drop of Ink: Nonlinear Impact of Misleading Information in Long-Context Reasoning

Vary the proportion of hard distractors in fixed-length context. Performance drops sharply within the first tiny fraction of distractors, then plateaus — a small amount of misleading material poisons the well almost completely. Attention analysis: hard distractors capture disproportionate attention even at low proportions.

Why you care: the result implies most "longer context = better" arguments are wrong in the presence of any adversarial or simply messy retrieval. RAG systems pay a steep tax for upstream retrieval imprecision that they can't recover from downstream. Filter ruthlessly.

Gao, Chen, Huang. Texas A&M. Published May 11, 2026.

🔮 ON THE HORIZON

The defender side of Glasswing is the next 90 days' story. If Mythos-class offensive capability has crossed the human-expert line, the matching defensive deployment is six to twelve months behind — most enterprise SOCs cannot ingest, triage, or act on a Mythos-volume output stream. Prediction: by Q1 2027, at least three of the twelve Glasswing launch partners ship branded "AI-native security operations" products. Whether the asymmetry actually flips defender-favorable depends on how fast the rest of the Fortune 1000 follows. The bet is reasonable; the execution is brutal.

🎯 WORTH WATCHING

OpenAI was second through the 32-step cyber-attack range, three weeks after Mythos. Their Glasswing-equivalent — if it exists — should surface in the next four weeks. If it doesn't, that's a story too.
